# Declarations, reviewers, and submission logistics

Open this when you need the declarations a cover letter should carry, suggested/excluded
reviewer handling, presubmission-enquiry format, or transfer/resubmission letters.

## Standard declarations

Most Nature-family submissions expect the cover letter (or the submission system) to
confirm:

- **Originality / non-dual-submission** — the work is original and not under consideration
  or published elsewhere. One sentence.
- **Author agreement** — all authors have seen and approved the submission. Confirm with the
  author; do not assert it for them.
- **Competing interests** — state any, or state none. If the author has not told you, flag
  it as author-input; do not write "none" by default.
- **Ethics / approvals** — for human, animal, or clinical work, note that approvals are in
  place and where they are described. Flag as author-input if unconfirmed; never invent an
  approval body or number.
- **Data / code availability** — a brief pointer if the journal expects it; route detail to
  nature-data.

Only state declarations the author has confirmed. A fabricated "no competing interests" or
"approved by all authors" is an integrity problem, not a formatting choice.

## Suggested and excluded reviewers

Many Nature-family journals invite (and some require) reviewer suggestions in the cover
letter or submission form.

- **Suggested reviewers:** name a few genuine experts who could assess the work fairly,
  with a one-line reason each (relevant expertise), and no undisclosed conflict with the
  authors. Do not invent names — if the author has not provided candidates, list this as
  author-input and optionally describe the *profile* of an ideal reviewer instead.
- **Excluded / opposed reviewers:** the author may name individuals to avoid (competitors,
  conflicts). State briefly and professionally ("to avoid a potential conflict of
  interest"), without disparagement. This is the author's call; never fabricate names.

## Presubmission enquiry

A presubmission enquiry asks the editor, before full submission, whether the work is likely
to be in scope. It is shorter than a cover letter:

- a one-line identification of the work and authors;
- the hook and the single key advance;
- one sentence of significance;
- a direct question: would the editor consider a full submission?
- no reviewer suggestions, no full declarations.

Keep it to a few short paragraphs. The editor is making a fast in-scope / out-of-scope call.

## Transfer and resubmission letters

- **Transfer** (journal offers to move a manuscript to a sister journal): briefly accept,
  restate the advance and why it fits the new journal's scope, and note that the manuscript
  is unchanged or summarise minor changes.
- **Resubmission after rejection-with-encouragement or revision-then-new-submission:**
  acknowledge prior interaction if relevant, state what changed, and re-make the fit case.
  Do not misrepresent a fresh submission as an invited resubmission, or vice versa — confirm
  the status with the author.

## What not to put in the letter

- The full abstract (the letter is an argument, not a summary).
- A list of every result and figure.
- Page-by-page manuscript detail.
- Anything the author has not confirmed (numbers, approvals, reviewer names, agreements).
