# Reporting standards: what each statement must contain

Open this when you need the governing Nature reporting-checklist rules, or the required
contents of a figure legend, Results statement, or Methods statistics paragraph.

## The governing checklist

Nature Portfolio requires a completed Reporting Summary (the Life Sciences / behavioural
Reporting Summary, or the field equivalent) for most research papers. The statistics
section of that checklist is the de facto contract for what reviewers expect. The recurring
demands are:

- The exact sample size (n) for each experimental group/condition, stated as a number.
- A statement of how the sample size was chosen (and whether a power analysis was used).
- Whether any data were excluded, and the pre-established criteria for exclusion.
- Whether the finding was reproduced (replication attempts and how many succeeded).
- Whether allocation was randomised, and the method.
- Whether investigators were blinded during allocation, outcome assessment, or analysis.
- For each statistical test: the test name, whether it was one- or two-sided, and whether
  the assumptions of the test were met.
- A clear definition of the centre and dispersion measures (mean/median; SD/SEM/CI).
- Exact p-values reported wherever possible (not just thresholds), and exact values for the
  test statistics where relevant.

A manuscript that cannot answer these for each claim is not yet submission-ready, and the
gaps are exactly what a referee will list.

## Figure legend — the compact line

Every panel with a statistical comparison or error bar needs, in the legend:

- what n is and its unit (e.g. "n = 6 biological replicates" / "n = 200 cells from 3
  independent experiments");
- the centre and dispersion ("data are mean ± s.e.m." / "boxes show median and IQR");
- the test name and sidedness for each comparison shown;
- the exact p-value, or a reference to a source-data table holding exact values; asterisk
  conventions (`*P < 0.05`) are acceptable as a visual aid only if the exact values are
  also available somewhere.

Template:
"Data are [mean ± s.e.m. / median and IQR]; n = [number] [unit] [from N independent
experiments]. [Comparison] by [test name], [one-/two-sided], P = [exact value]."

## Results sentence — magnitude first

In running text, lead with the effect and its uncertainty, then the test and p:
"X was [magnitude: fold/difference/HR] ([95% CI lower–upper]; [test], P = [exact])."
Significance alone ("X was significantly higher") without magnitude is a weak claim.

## Methods statistics paragraph — the design record

A dedicated statistics subsection in Methods should state:

- the software and version used for analysis;
- how n and the replication scheme were defined across the study;
- the tests used and the reason each was appropriate (including assumption checks and any
  transformation);
- how multiple comparisons were handled;
- the significance threshold used and that exact p-values are reported;
- randomisation, blinding, and exclusion criteria (or an explicit statement that they were
  not applicable, with reason).

Template:
"Statistical analyses were performed in [software vX]. [Replicate/n definition.] [Test
choice and justification, including assumption checks.] [Multiple-comparison handling.]
[Two-sided] tests were used and exact P-values are reported; P < [threshold] was considered
significant. [Randomisation / blinding / exclusion statements.]"
