# Source Basis

Use this file when a user asks why a rule exists, wants primary-source justification, or
needs to audit the `nature-stats` skill against real reporting guidance.

## Source map

| Skill rule | Primary support |
|---|---|
| A completed Reporting Summary with a statistics section is expected for most research papers. | Nature Portfolio requires authors to complete a Reporting Summary (Life Sciences / behavioural and social sciences, or field equivalent) covering statistics, replication, randomisation, and blinding. |
| State exact sample size (n) per group and what one unit of n is. | Nature reporting standards and the Reporting Summary require the exact sample size for each experimental group and a clear statement of the experimental unit. |
| Report exact p-values rather than thresholds. | Nature statistics reporting guidance asks authors to give exact p-values wherever possible, down to a journal floor, instead of only inequalities such as P < 0.05. |
| Define centre and dispersion (mean/median, s.d./s.e.m./CI) for every error bar. | Nature reporting standards require error bars and summary statistics to be defined, including whether dispersion is s.d., s.e.m., or a confidence interval. |
| Name the test, its sidedness, and whether assumptions were met. | The Reporting Summary statistics checklist asks for the test used, whether tests were one- or two-sided, and whether the assumptions of the test were met. |
| Report effect sizes and confidence intervals, not significance alone. | Wider statistical-reporting guidance (and the trend in Nature-family reporting) emphasises effect estimates with confidence intervals over p-value-only reporting. |
| Distinguish biological from technical replicates and avoid pseudoreplication. | Nature reporting standards and statistics commentary stress that the unit of replication must be defined and that technical replicates are not independent samples. |
| Control and report multiple comparisons. | Standard statistical practice and Nature reporting guidance require multiple-comparison handling to be stated (correction method and the family it applies to). |
| Report randomisation, blinding, and exclusion criteria. | The Reporting Summary asks whether allocation was randomised, whether investigators were blinded, and what data-exclusion criteria were pre-established. |

## Notes for future updates

  fields add specific statistics requirements (e.g. clinical, ecology, genomics).
- Verify the current Reporting Summary version and the exact-p journal floor before giving
  final submission advice; both can change.
- Keep this file a source map, not a policy mirror. Link to official pages rather than
  copying full text, and confirm any URL that may have moved.
